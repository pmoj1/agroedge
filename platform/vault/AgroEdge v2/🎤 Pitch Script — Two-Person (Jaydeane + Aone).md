## [0:00] Hook — Jaydeane

"Across Africa, smallholder farmers grow most of the food — and almost none of them know if they made a profit last season. No records, no credit, no timely advice. They farm blind.

Dozens of apps have tried to fix this by selling software to farmers. Almost all of them failed — for one reason: the farmers who have the problem can't afford to pay.

AgroEdge solves that. We don't charge the farmer. We get governments to pay, and the farmer gets the system free."

## [0:45] The problem — Jaydeane

"Let me make it concrete. 95% of African farmers are rain-fed — one bad season wipes them out. Up to 40% of crops are lost to pests and disease — that's a 66-billion-dollar hole every year in Africa alone. Another 30 to 40% of what survives is lost after harvest. And when farmers finally sell, they keep just 5 to 12 cents of every dollar the food is worth.

Underneath all of it is one root problem: no data. No records means no budgeting, no proof of income, and no access to credit. That's the problem we attack first."

## [1:30] The insight — Jaydeane

"Here's the insight the failed apps missed. The right question isn't *'what problem do farmers have?'* It's *'what problem will someone with a budget pay to solve?'*

And the answer is sitting right there: governments are spending real money to register and support these exact farmers. Kenya has digitally registered 6.9 million farmers. Zimbabwe's input scheme reaches 3.5 million households. Botswana has 105,000 on a government system already.

But every one of those systems stops at registration. They know *who* the farmers are. They can't turn that into decisions. That gap is our business."

## [2:15] The solution — Aone

"That's where AgroEdge comes in. We sit on top of the registry the government already has, and we add the intelligence layer they're missing.

A farmer — with help from an extension officer — logs activity, expenses and income. We turn that into a dashboard and AI insights: cost optimisation, profitability, productivity advice, and alerts. Timely pest alerts alone have been shown to lift smallholder income 18 to 26%.

And the data compounds. After a season of records, we can generate a credit-readiness score — which makes a previously un-bankable farmer lendable. That's the asset lenders and insurers will pay for. We're not replacing the registry; we're the brain on top of it."

## [3:15] Why now — Aone

"Three things make this the moment. One — governments are digitising farmer data right now, and the AI layer is the obvious next step they haven't built. Two — AI is finally cheap enough to deliver per-farmer at scale. Three — donor and government budgets are actively funding this through the AfDB, FAO and World Bank. The rails are being laid; we ride on them."

## [3:45] Market — Jaydeane

"The financing gap for smallholders in sub-Saharan Africa is around 117 billion dollars a year. And yet only about 170 million dollars went into African agtech last year — down 20%. So you have an enormous problem and capital pulling back. That's exactly the white space for a capital-efficient model that doesn't burn cash acquiring farmers one by one.

Our near-term market is national agricultural programmes and ministries across our ten target countries — government systems that already aggregate millions of farmers."

## [4:15] Go-to-market — Jaydeane

"We're not boiling the ocean. Two first doors. Botswana — whose Temo Letlotlo programme funds its own agriculture, so there's no donor dependency, and already runs a 105,000-farmer system that has no intelligence layer. And Zimbabwe — the biggest need, where we have home advantage, entering through the donor-funded registry program.

We've already drafted government concept notes for both, and scoped a small paid pilot in each — one district or province, one season. That's how we prove it without waiting years for a national tender."

## [5:00] Traction & roadmap — Aone

"Where we are: the MVP is in active build — farmer profile, logging, dashboard, AI insights. Next is moving off no-code onto a scalable stack and building the credit-scoring engine. The roadmap then adds weather and SMS/WhatsApp so we reach farmers who don't have smartphones, and later disease detection and satellite imagery."

## [5:30] Team — both

Jaydeane: "I'm CEO and product lead — I own product, partnerships, and the numbers; I built our 36-month model."

Aone: "I'm CTO and AI lead — I own the architecture and the AI engine."

Jaydeane: "Two founders, fully focused, African-built for African farmers."

## [6:00] The ask — Jaydeane

"We're raising `US$[amount]` to do three things: move off no-code and build the AI and credit layer, deliver the Botswana and Zimbabwe pilots, and stand up the data infrastructure that powers credit scoring. That capital takes us from a working MVP to two live government pilots and our first revenue."

## [6:30] Close — Jaydeane

"Governments already know who the farmers are. AgroEdge turns that into decisions — and the farmer pays nothing, because the government does. Smarter farms. Stronger futures. We'd love to have you in on this."

## Q&A — prepared answers

**"Why won't this fail like every other farmer app?"**

> Because we don't depend on farmers paying or even owning a smartphone. The institution pays, and delivery runs through extension officers who are already in the field.
> 

**"Won't FAO or the World Bank just build this themselves?"**

> They build the *registry* — the base layer. They don't build the AI/credit intelligence on top, and their systems are explicitly designed for third parties to plug into. We're a partner to that infrastructure, not a competitor.
> 

**"How do you actually make money?"**

> Governments pay per-farmer or per-programme for the platform and reporting. The high-margin layer is credit-readiness scoring that lenders and insurers pay for once we have data history.
> 

**"You're on Bubble — is that a risk?"**

> It's deliberate: no-code let us validate the workflow cheaply. Part of this raise moves us to a scalable stack now that the model is proven.
> 

**"Government deals take forever. How do you survive that?"**

> We lead with self-funded Botswana and small paid pilots, not national tenders. The pilots generate revenue and proof while the bigger deals mature.
> 

**"What's the moat?"**

> The farmer financial data we accumulate. Once we hold the records that make farmers lendable, we're the layer every lender and program wants to integrate — and that data can't be copied.
> 

**"Why you two?"**

> Product + finance in one founder, AI + engineering in the other, both African and close to the market. Lean, fast, and building for a market we understand.
