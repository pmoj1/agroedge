# Pitch Numbers — Market, Unit Economics & Revenue Model

> **TL;DR for the investor:** There are already **~141,600 farmers registered** in government systems across Botswana and Zimbabwe — onboarded and paid for by the state, not us. Serving just that existing base is a **~$425K–$708K/year platform business** at $3–$5 per farmer. Add the credit-readiness layer and the per-farmer value roughly doubles. And that's before the real prize: Zimbabwe's input scheme alone covers **3.5M households**, so these two markets carry a **~$14M ARR ceiling** as digitisation expands.
> 

## Assumptions (stated up front)

- **Registered-farmer counts are real and sourced** (government / FAO figures). Everything else is modelled on top of them.
- **Price per farmer/year:** Conservative **$3** · Base **$4** · Upside **$5**. Anchored in African agri-data pricing norms ($2–$15/farmer/yr); a government volume buyer sits at the low-to-mid end. *— hypothesis, to confirm with a real buyer.*
- **Cost-to-serve per farmer/year:** assumed **$1** for margin illustration. *— replace with the figure from the 36-month model.*
- **Revenue = farmers served × price per farmer/year.** Simple, defensible, easy to stress-test live.

## A. The real addressable base (registered farmers today)

| Market | Registered now | Wider programme (expansion TAM) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Botswana — Temo Letlotlo CMS | **105,000** | 105,000 (national arable base) |
| Zimbabwe — IDEA registry | **36,588** *(14,915 already onboarded)* | **3.5M households** (Pfumvudza/Intwasa) |
| **Combined** | **~141,588** | **~3.6M+** |

The investor takeaway: the hardest, most expensive part of agritech — getting farmers identified and onboarded — **has already been done and funded by the government.** We plug into it.

## B. Annual revenue if we serve the existing registered base (SAM)

| Price / farmer / yr | Botswana (105,000) | Zimbabwe (36,588) | **Combined ARR** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $3 | $315,000 | $109,764 | **$424,764** |
| $4 *(base)* | $420,000 | $146,352 | **$566,352** |
| $5 | $525,000 | $182,940 | **$707,940** |

A **half-million-dollar ARR business** lives inside just the *already-registered* farmers of two small markets — no new farmer acquisition required.

## C. The realistic 3-year ramp (SOM — base case, $4/farmer/yr)

We don't capture the whole base on day one. This is the grounded path:

| Stage | Farmers served | % of registered base | Platform revenue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Year 1 — Pilots** | 2,000 | ~1.4% | ~$8,000 *(2 pilots, priced near cost to win references)* |
| **Year 2 — Expansion** | ~28,000 | ~20% | ~$112,000 |
| **Year 3 — Scale** | ~78,000 | ~55% | ~$312,000 |

Year 1 is deliberately tiny and cheap — it buys the reference customers and the data, not revenue. The jump from Year 2 to Year 3 is converting pilots into full programme contracts.

## D. The credit-readiness upside (the high-margin second stream)

Once farm data compounds, lenders/insurers pay for credit-readiness scores — they earn on the loan, so they pay for data that lowers default. **Illustrative, Year 3:**

| Line | Value *(illustrative)* |
| --- | --- |
| Farmers on platform (Year 3) | 78,000 |
| Share scored / loan-linked | 30% → 23,400 farmers |
| Fee per scored farmer (or per loan facilitated) | $5 |
| **Credit-layer revenue** | **~$117,000** |
| **Year 3 total (platform + credit)** | **~$429,000** |

This is the lever that lifts revenue *per farmer* without adding a single new farmer.

## F. The ceiling — why this isn't a small business

The registered base is just the entry point. Zimbabwe's Pfumvudza scheme covers **3.5M households**; with Botswana that's **~3.6M farmers** in these two markets alone. At **$4/farmer/yr**:

| Penetration of the 3.6M Zim + Botswana base | Farmers | ARR @ $4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5% | 180,000 | **$720,000** |
| 10% | 360,000 | **$1,440,000** |
| 25% | 900,000 | **$3,600,000** |
| 50% | 1,800,000 | **$7,200,000** |
| 100% | 3,600,000 | **$14,400,000** |

And that's **2 of our 10 target markets.** Across all ten (tens of millions of smallholders), 5M farmers at $4 = **$20M ARR** — before the credit layer.

## What this tells the investor (the 4 takeaways)

1. **Demand is de-risked.** 141,600 farmers are already registered and government-funded — we don't have to prove farmers will sign up.
2. **Real revenue exists in the existing base** — ~$425K–$708K ARR from already-registered farmers in two small markets.
3. **Revenue per farmer compounds** via the credit-readiness layer — roughly doubling value without new acquisition.
4. **The ceiling is large** — a ~$14M ARR opportunity in just these two markets at full digitisation; $20M+ across all ten.

## Caveats / to confirm before pitch day

- **Price per farmer is a hypothesis** — validate $3–$5 with one real ministry / national-programme officer.
- **Cost-to-serve** — drop in the real figure from the 36-month model to finalise margins.
- **Zimbabwe registered count (36,588)** is the current IDEA rollout — it grows each season toward the 3.5M scheme.
- **Credit-layer assumptions** (30% scored, $5/farmer) are illustrative placeholders — refine once a lender conversation is live.
