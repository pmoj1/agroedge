**One-liner:** AgroEdge turns African farm data into smarter decisions — and we get governments and partners to pay, so the farmer uses it free.

## 2. The 30-second version

Most African farmers fly blind: no records, no credit, no timely advice. Apps built for them fail because farmers can't pay. AgroEdge flips the model — we sit on top of the farmer registries governments and NGOs are already building, turn that data into AI insights and credit-readiness, and the *institution* pays while the farmer gets it free. Our first two doors are the Botswana and Zimbabwe ministries of agriculture.

## 3. The problem — in numbers

```
AFRICA'S SMALLHOLDER REALITY
Rain-fed, no irrigation      95%  ███████████████████
Crops lost to pests/weeds    40%  ████████
Produce lost post-harvest    35%  ███████
Farmer's cut of final price   8%  ██
```

- ~**95%** of African farmers depend on rain-fed agriculture — fully exposed to erratic weather.
- Up to **40%** of crops lost to pests, disease and weeds; invasive pests alone cost Africa ~**US$66B/year**.
- **30–40%** of produce lost post-harvest.
- Farmers receive only **5–12%** of the final consumer price — middlemen and information gaps take the rest.
- Most have **no records**, so they can't budget, can't prove income, and can't get credit.

## 4. The opportunity

```
THE OPPORTUNITY  (US$ per year)
Smallholder financing gap, SSA   $117B  ████████████████████
Crop losses to pests/disease      $66B  ███████████
African agtech funding (2025)    $0.17B  ▏ ← capital pulled back ~20%; the gap is wide open
```

The pain is enormous and the financing gap is ~**US$117B** in sub-Saharan Africa — yet only ~**US$170M** went into African agtech in 2025 (down ~20%). Huge problem, thin competition for capital = white space for a capital-efficient, partner-funded model.

## 6. Why now

- Governments are **actively digitising** farmer registries right now (Kenya 6.9M, Zimbabwe IDEA live 2025, Botswana CMS live 2023) — but they stop at registration. The **AI/advisory/credit layer is missing** — that's us.
- AI insight is finally cheap enough to deliver per-farmer at scale.
- Donor + government budgets are flowing into exactly this (AfDB, FAO, World Bank, EU).
- EU Deforestation Regulation is forcing coffee/cocoa traceability — a funded, deadline-driven wedge.

## 7. Business model — who actually pays

B2B2F: institutions pay, farmers use free. Sequenced by who pays fastest → highest value:

1. **NGO / government programs** — pay now for beneficiary data + reporting (our MVP exactly).
2. **Cooperatives** — scaled distribution; one deal onboards hundreds of farmers.
3. **Lenders / insurers** — highest value; pay for credit-readiness scoring once we have data history.

## 13. Risks & mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Farmers can't/won't use an app | We never rely on direct adoption — delivery is through extension officers & partners |
| Government sales cycles are long | Lead with self-funded Botswana; run a small paid pilot, not a national tender |
| FAO/World Bank own the registry | We don't compete with the registry — we're the layer on top, interoperable |
| Credit-scoring needs data history | Phase it: NGO/govt data first → scoring later → lenders pay last |
| Single-country concentration | Two parallel beachheads (Botswana + Zimbabwe) |
