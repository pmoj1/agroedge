# B2G Country Read — Zimbabwe · Botswana · Mozambique

> **TL;DR** — The national farmer-registry slot is already taken or being taken in all three countries (by government or FAO). So AgroEdge does **not** pitch "we'll register your farmers." It pitches the **AI insights / financial analytics / credit-readiness layer that sits on top** of the registry they already have. On that basis: **Botswana** is the best first target (pays from its own budget), **Zimbabwe** the strong alternative (biggest need + home advantage), and **Mozambique** is later-only (donor-funded, Portuguese, politically unstable).
> 

## The one rule that applies to all three

Don't compete to *be* the registry — FAO + World Bank + national governments win that, usually with donor money, for free. Sell the value-added layer the registries lack: AI insights, expense/income analytics, advisory, and loan/insurance-readiness scoring, plugged into the government's farmer data backbone.

Cross-cutting adoption fact: an entry-level phone costs 70%+ of a farmer's monthly income in sub-Saharan Africa, and most ag apps have under 30% active users. The route to the farmer runs through the government program and its extension officers — not an app download.

## Zimbabwe — biggest need + home advantage

- **Existing:** FAO's IDEA registry (phase 2, Oct 2025, AfDB-funded under ZEFPP) registering ~36.6k farmers across 12 districts / 4 provinces. Separate TIMB biometric tobacco-grower registry. National AgriTech Strategy 2021–2025 (expiring → successor likely).
- **The gap:** IDEA covers a sliver of the 3.5M households in the Pfumvudza/Intwasa input scheme. Fragmented (tobacco is separate). No financial/AI layer.
- **Entry angle:** Value-added layer on IDEA/Pfumvudza, or one province or value chain, or partner into the AfDB/FAO program rather than compete with it.
- **Why it's attractive:** English, active digitization push, Ministry actively seeking tools, likely founder networks.
- **Watch:** ZiG currency instability, thin public finances — so lean toward a donor-funded entry, not a cash tender.

## Botswana — the one that pays from its own pocket  ✅ best first target

- **Existing:** Temo Letlotlo (crops, launched Oct 2023) with a government-built digital Crop Management System — 105k+ farmers already registered. Thuo Letlotlo for livestock. 100% subsidy for micro-scale farmers.
- **The gap:** The CMS does registration and admin only. The commercialisation goal ("output-based, wealth creation, self-reliance") needs exactly the AI insights, advisory, and credit layer AgroEdge builds.
- **Entry angle:** Direct proposal / procurement for the value-added layer. Small farmer base = a national pilot is realistic.
- **Why it's the best first target:** Funds itself (diamond economy, no donor dependency = real "willing to pay"), English-speaking, manageable scale.
- **Watch:** Historic change of government (Oct 2024) — Temo Letlotlo was a previous-administration flagship and may be under review. Position as helping the **new** government improve/modernise it. Small population caps total contract size.

## Mozambique — biggest scale, hardest entry  ⏸ defer

- **Existing:** SUSTENTA (World Bank flagship linking smallholders to lead farmers/cooperatives) and an FAO/EU e-voucher scheme (since 2015). 3.2M smallholders = 95% of production; only ~6.5% belong to farmer organisations (weak aggregation = hard distribution). No national MIS yet.
- **The gap:** Greenfield on the registry — but FAO is the obvious front-runner to build it (they ran the Kenya study tour and own KIAMIS/IDEA elsewhere).
- **Entry angle:** Only via a donor/NGO program (World Bank SUSTENTA, EU, FAO). Not a direct government play.
- **Why defer:** Portuguese-language barrier (real cost for an English team/MVP), serious political instability since the disputed Oct 2024 election, lowest digital infrastructure of the three, donor-only funding.

## Recommendation & sequence

1. **Lead with Botswana** — it's the cleanest "government pays, farmers get it free" fit, English, small enough to pilot nationally. Build the proposal around being the AI/advisory/credit layer on top of the Temo Letlotlo CMS, framed as helping the new administration commercialise the program.
2. **Run Zimbabwe in parallel** — biggest need and home advantage; enter via the AfDB/FAO IDEA program or a single province/value chain, since the public purse is thin.
3. **Mozambique later** — only through a World Bank/EU/FAO program, and only once you can absorb the language and stability costs.

Consistent pitch across all three: *"Your registry knows who the farmers are. We turn that into decisions — insights, advisory, and credit-readiness — and the farmer gets it free because you fund it."*
