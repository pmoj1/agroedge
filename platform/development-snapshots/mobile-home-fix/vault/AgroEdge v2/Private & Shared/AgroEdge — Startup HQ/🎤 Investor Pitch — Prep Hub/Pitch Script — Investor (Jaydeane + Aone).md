## 1. Hook — [SLIDE 1: Title]

**J:** "Across Africa, smallholder farmers feed the continent — they're about 80% of the farms and produce most of the food. But almost all of them farm blind: no records, no data, no idea if they made or lost money this season. We're AgroEdge, and we turn farm data into smarter decisions."

*Delivery: slow, confident. Let "farm blind" land.*

## 2. The problem — [SLIDE 2]

**J:** "That blindness is expensive. Because farmers have no financial record, banks won't lend to them. Because they have no agronomy support, pests and disease wipe out up to 40% of crops — about 66 billion dollars a year across Africa. And because they have no market data, they capture as little as 5 to 12 cents of every dollar their produce sells for. The problems are real, documented, and huge."

## 3. The insight — [SLIDE 3]

**J:** "Here's what most agritech gets wrong. The farmers who feel these problems most can't pay to solve them — that's why farmer-facing apps keep dying. So we asked a sharper question: who *will* pay to fix a farmer's problem? The answer is governments. Across Africa, national agricultural programmes already spend billions supporting these same farmers — and they're doing it blind on the data. That reframes everything."

*Delivery: this is the differentiator. Pause after "can't pay."*

## 4. Solution — [SLIDE 4]

**A:** "AgroEdge is an AI-powered farm-management platform with two faces. To the farmer, it's dead-simple: log your activity, costs, and income, and get back clear insights — what's costing you, what's profitable, what to do next. To the government programme behind them, it's something more valuable: a clean, structured data trail on every farmer they fund."

## 5. How the AI works — [SLIDE 5]

**A:** "Under the hood: each farmer has a profile and a farm; they log activities, expenses, and income. Our AI layer turns that into cost-optimisation, profitability analysis, productivity recommendations, and alerts — including pest alerts, which on their own are shown to lift farmer income 18 to 26%. And as that data accumulates, it becomes a credit-readiness score — which is the part institutions pay real money for."

## 6. Business model — [SLIDE 6]

**J:** "So we don't charge farmers. We're B2G — business to government. The national programme or ministry of agriculture pays; the farmer uses it free. We sell them the intelligence layer their programme is missing: a clean data trail on every farmer they fund, M&E and impact reporting, and over time a credit-readiness engine that de-risks lending. One government contract puts us in front of an entire country's farmers."

## 7. Go-to-market — [SLIDE 7]

**J:** "Our wedge is government. Across Africa, ministries are building national farmer registries — Kenya has 6.9 million farmers on one already, funding free inputs off the back of it. We don't compete with that registry. We're the intelligence layer that sits on top of it. We've already drafted concept notes for two ministries — Botswana, whose Temo Letlotlo programme funds its own farmers and already has about 105,000 of them on a crop-management system, and Zimbabwe, where we'd enter through an existing donor-funded registry. Pilots first, then scale."

## 8. Market — [SLIDE 8]

**J:** "The base is enormous: 3.5 million farming households in Zimbabwe's input scheme, 3.2 million smallholders in Mozambique, and in Botswana the Temo Letlotlo programme already pays to support its own farmers, with about 105,000 registered. The financing gap we help unlock is over 117 billion dollars in sub-Saharan Africa alone. And the B2G model is proven — Kenya runs 6.9 million farmers on a government registry — we're not inventing it, we're bringing the missing intelligence layer to it."

## 9. Why we win — [SLIDE 9]

**A:** "Three moats. One: we ride registries instead of rebuilding them — faster and cheaper than anyone starting from scratch. Two: a data flywheel — every season of farmer data makes our credit scoring sharper and harder to copy. Three: distribution — we reach farmers through institutions and their extension officers, which solves the adoption problem that kills consumer agritech apps."

## 10. Team — [SLIDE 10]

**J:** "I'm Jaydeane, CEO and product lead — I run the product and the numbers; I'm also a finance student, which is why this is built around farm economics."

**A:** "I'm Aone, CTO and AI lead — I own the platform and the AI insight engine. Between us: the farmer-finance product and the AI to power it."

## 11. The ask + close — [SLIDE 10]

**J:** "We're raising **$[amount]** to close our first government pilots in Botswana and Zimbabwe, ship MVP v1, and build the data foundation for credit scoring. We want to be the operating system for African agriculture — and the way in is the data layer nobody else is building. Smarter farms. Stronger futures."

## Anticipated investor questions + answers

**Q: Smallholders can't pay — how do you actually make money?**

Governments pay, not farmers. National agricultural programmes already budget to support these farmers; we sell them the data and intelligence layer their programme lacks — M&E and impact reporting now, credit-readiness scoring later. The farmer data is the asset; de-risking the programme's spend is what's valuable.

**Q: Government sales are slow — won't that kill your runway?**

We don't bet runway on a national tender. We start with small, fundable pilots inside programmes that already have their own budget — Botswana's Temo Letlotlo funds its own farmers — and enter others through existing donor-funded registries. Government is the scale prize; pilots are the first dollar.

**Q: FAO and KIAMIS already build registries — why you?**

We don't compete with the registry — those are donor-funded public infrastructure and we'd lose that fight. We're the intelligence and credit layer on top, which they explicitly don't build. We're interoperable, not duplicative.

**Q: Farmers can't use apps — how do you get adoption?**

We reach farmers through the government programme and its extension officers, not app-store downloads. SMS/USSD/WhatsApp are on the roadmap for low-end phones. Programme-led delivery is the whole point of B2G.

**Q: What stops a competitor copying you?**

The data flywheel — proprietary, multi-season farm-financial data that makes our credit scoring better the longer we run — plus the institutional relationships and local execution. Copy the app, not the data.

**Q: Why build on Bubble — is it scalable?**

Bubble lets us validate the product and win pilots now, fast and cheap. Once validated, we migrate to our production stack [Next.js / FastAPI / Supabase / Africa's Talking for SMS/USSD]. Right tool for the stage.

**Q: Who are your competitors?**

Apollo and ThriveAgric — but they're asset-heavy, bundling credit and physical inputs. We're the lighter software/data layer, programme-led and government-first. We can power lenders rather than become one.

**Q: What's your traction today?**

Honest answer: MVP in build, a full 36-month financial model, and two government concept notes ready to go [+ any LOIs / live conversations]. We're pre-revenue and raising to convert the pipeline into pilots.

**Q: What's the biggest risk?**

Distribution and government sales cycles. We mitigate with pilot-first entry, donor-funded routes, and NGO/co-op deals that pay sooner. We'd rather under-promise on timelines than over-promise.
